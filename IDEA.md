# The Other Script — Idea Lock (Phase 1)

**Working title:** The Other Script  
**Primary user:** People leadership / talent partners who review, compile, synthesize, and decide after partners discuss a consultant’s promotion  
**Tier aim:** Tier 3 (do the lived problem justice; evidence over slogans)

## One-liner

A promotion-synthesis stress tester that shows people leadership the script they’re about to ratify — and the other script that fits the same evidence — so bias can’t hide in soft language on the way to a decision.

## Core insight

Bias rarely shows up as one sexist sentence. It shows up as different language for the same performance — and it often hardens at the synthesis step, when messy partner talk becomes one official story.

## Why it matters (literature + data for intro / report)

Use these in the app intro, report Motivation, and slides — cite sources; don’t invent extra numbers.

**The promotion gap is structural**
- For the 11th year running, women face a “broken rung” at the first step to manager: for every **100 men** promoted to manager, only **93 women** are — and fewer women of color (**82** Asian women/Latinas, **60** Black women) (Lean In & McKinsey, *Women in the Workplace 2025*).
- Women hold ~**49%** of entry-level roles but only ~**42%** of manager roles; men outnumber women at manager level and the gap compounds upward (same source).
- Only **30%** of entry-level women report a promotion in the last two years vs **43%** of entry-level men (WiW 2025).

**The mechanism is language, not one slur**
- Stanford / Correll et al.: in real Fortune 500 performance reviews, gender shapes what managers *notice* and what they *value* — e.g. women more often viewed as communal, but communal language does not drive top ratings; “taking charge” links to top ratings for men more than for women (*American Sociological Review*, 2020; Stanford GSB summary).
- Classic evaluation bias: men more often judged on **potential**, women on **past achievements**; women get less credit for success and more criticism for failure (McKinsey research on women in the workplace).
- Textio (large-scale review language studies): women receive **~22% more personality-focused feedback** than men — even among high performers; personality talk is less actionable for advancement. Stereotyped labels skew hard (e.g. women far more often “abrasive”/“opinionated”; men more often “confident”/“ambitious”) (*Language Bias in Performance Feedback*).

**Why The Other Script**
- Existing tools often score resumes or coach the writer. The high-leverage moment in consulting is when people leadership **synthesizes partner talk into a decision**. That is where soft scripts get ratified.
- We don’t output a discrimination verdict. We force a dual read: the script about to be locked vs the other script the same evidence supports.

## Workplace flow we design for

1. Partners discuss a consultant’s promotion (notes / chat / nomination talk).  
2. People leadership reviews, compiles, synthesizes.  
3. People leadership decides.  
**The app sits at steps 2–3.**

## Product shape (hybrid)

- **Stress-test:** dual scripts (current synthesis vs evidence-first / identity-minimized other script) + evidence gaps  
- **Light calibration:** replay partner-room archetypes (advocate, skeptic, “presence/culture” voice) with a chair that only lets evidence through  
- **Slate mode (stretch / Tier 3):** compare narrative patterns across multiple cases in one cycle  

## Gender posture (balance)

- **Report / motivation:** name gender bias in promotions plainly; ground it in lived experience **and** the literature above.  
- **App UX:** not branded as a “Gender Bias Detector.” Named and framed as The Other Script, with a research-backed intro.  
- **Engine + control:** always-on evidence/other-script core; **Gender-pattern stress test** is a prominent toggle (default ON for demo, user can turn off). When ON, Language Pattern Analyst + Delta call out gendered pattern hits explicitly.  
- **Claims:** never “this was discrimination.” Always “scripts diverge / evidence is thin / ask these questions back.”

## What we will not build

- A generic resume/PDF chatbot  
- A single bias score out of 100 as the main output  
- Legal conclusions or HR “findings” of discrimination
